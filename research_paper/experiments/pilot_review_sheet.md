## How to review
Each expected answer was produced automatically by a written rule applied to a named source. For each item, decide whether the expected answer is right for the input:
- **Accept:** the expected answer is right.
- **Reject:** it is wrong. Say what it should be and why.
- **Uncertain:** you cannot decide. Say why.

You do **not** need customs or tariff knowledge, and you must **not** assign HS codes. Product items ask only *which of the 60 catalogue product types is meant, or none* (list: `pilot/catalogue_base_types.csv`). Country items ask *which country is meant*, and whether LEXITAX has tax rates for it (`taxes.csv`, 40 countries).

**Two different things are recorded for every item:**
- **Gold answer:** what the input really means, independent of any system.
- **Expected system behaviour:** what a *safe* assistive system should do with it. A system that cannot recognise a valid input and declines is *safe but incomplete* ("over-abstention"). Only a wrong answer given without warning is an error ("silent error").

You are judging the gold answer and the expected safe behaviour. You see no system outputs.

## After the review
| Outcome | Action |
|---|---|
| ≥ 9 Accept, and any Reject concerns a single item | The labelling rules are accepted |
| A Reject shows a **rule** is wrong | The rule is fixed, the draft rebuilt, the change logged, and a replacement item for that stratum is shown to Author 1 |
| ≥ 2 rule-level Rejects | Stop and review the design with the authors |

This is a single-reviewer sanity check. **No inter-annotator agreement is computed or claimed.**
