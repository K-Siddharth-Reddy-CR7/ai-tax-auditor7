## 1. Why the product set was redesigned
v2 had 6,096 product items. 4,676 of them (77 %) were formal HS descriptions from unrelated chapters. Pooled results would therefore have measured mostly whether a system refuses tariff legalese, which is not the research question.

In v3:
- formal HS texts are capped at 243 items in their own category, **P8**, excluded from pooled product results;
- the realistic categories P1–P7 hold 612 items and are designed around specific behaviours: identification, robustness, confusion, ambiguity and out-of-catalogue refusal.

## 2. Sizing principle
Sizes are set by what each category can **independently** contain, not by a target total.

| Factor | How it limits size |
|---|---|
| **Catalogue-derived categories (P1–P5)** | Bounded by the 60 product types. Extra variants of the same type are correlated (same cluster) and add little information. The design uses ≤ 2 items per type, and analysis uses **cluster-level** statistics (cluster = product type). |
| **External-source categories (P7, P8)** | Seeded random samples sized for a worst-case 95 % CI half-width of about ±0.09 (n≈118 test) and ±0.07 (n≈171 test). |
| **Rule-enumerated categories (P4 short forms, P6 ambiguous)** | Contain *every* item the rule produces (populations, not samples). They are small by nature, so their CIs are wide and they are reported descriptively. |
| **Split** | Group-level 30 % DEV / 70 % TEST (groups = HS-4 heading; ambiguity word; Open Products Facts parent category). DEV is used only to select S1's three parameters. |

## 4. Country benchmark (2,122 items; mostly populations)
Every item carries separate fields:
- `expected_iso` (identification);
- `tax_data_available` (one of the 40 table countries);
- `form` (how the string was made);
- **`s1_builtin`**: whether the exact normalised string is in S1's own alias table.

| Subset | Items (test) | Source | s1_builtin | Role |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| J1 Table names | 120 (81) | 40 table names + case/whitespace noise | **yes** | Sanity; by construction for S1 |
| J2 ISO codes | 498 (344) | Alpha-2 and alpha-3 of all 249 ISO countries | **yes** | By construction for S1; *informative for S0* (two-letter codes may trigger S0's substring rule) |
| J3 ISO names | 386 (272) | ISO name/official/common of all 249 | **yes** | By construction for S1; includes the 209 unsupported countries (S0's Niger→Nigeria type errors) |
| J4 Typos | 247 (170) | One seeded edit per country; rejected if it equals any alias | no | **Held-out**: robustness |
| J5 CLDR names | 821 (564) | Unicode CLDR 47 names in de/fr/es/it/nl/pt; never part of S1's table; cross-country collisions removed | no | **Held-out**: generalisation |
| J6 Ambiguous words | 16 (10) | Words in ≥ 2 ISO names that are not names themselves (Korea, South, Sint…) | no | Must not identify |
| J7 Regions | 34 (26) | CLDR non-country regions (Europe, Caribbean…) | no | Must not identify |

**Why populations, not samples.** ISO forms and CLDR names are finite, enumerable lists. Using all of them removes any sampling choice that could favour one system. The large size does not inflate claims, because:
- results are always reported per subset, and pooled country results include **only held-out forms (J4–J7)**;
- clusters are countries (ISO code), so CIs reflect about 150–170 countries, not hundreds of strings.

**How we avoid overstating S1:**
- S1 resolves J1–J3 by table lookup, so its accuracy there is ~100 % by design. Those rows are labelled "by construction" and never used for claims.
- Generalisation claims use J4–J7 only.
- Identification and tax behaviour are separate tasks: identifying Algeria correctly is CORRECT for identification even though the tool must then refuse.

## 5. Human verification needed (and why automation cannot do it)
| Step | Items | Why a human is needed | Time |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pilot | 10 | Sanity-check that each labelling rule yields sensible expectations | 15–20 min, 1 reviewer |
| Look-alike confirmation | 60 (P7 look-alikes) | Whether e.g. "Motor Vehicle Parking Cameras" is outside the catalogue type "Video Camera" is a semantic judgement no rule settles | 15–20 min, 1 reviewer |
| Optional 60-item audit | 60 | Deferred. Needed only if the pilot finds a rule-level problem in a category | — |

If the authors prefer zero human labelling beyond the pilot, the 60 look-alikes can be dropped, leaving P7 with 100 plain items. The paper then loses its hardest out-of-catalogue test, and the decision will be recorded.

## 6. Remaining design limitations
- Typos and formatting variants are synthetic. Their error distribution may differ from real data entry.
- P4 short forms are rule-generated and their realism is unverified, so only wrong codes are scored.
- P5 has 12 test clusters and P6 has 11 test items, so CIs are wide.
- Open Products Facts names are category names, not invoice lines.
- The catalogue's HS assignments are unverified (`pilot/catalogue_base_types.csv` supports an optional plausibility check).
